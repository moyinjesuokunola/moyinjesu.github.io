The idea

The first resource problem is often visibility.

A business can own useful knowledge without documenting it. A community can have skilled people without knowing who they are. A household can have equipment that is rarely used. An organization can have information scattered across notebooks, phones, people, folders, and conversations.

Resource Intelligence creates a disciplined way to find those resources, describe them accurately, understand where they are, and establish what condition they are in. It is not simply a list of possessions. It is a structured view of what can contribute to a goal.

01 / SEE

Look beyond objects.

People, skills, time, relationships, information, space, tools, money, and knowledge can all be resources.

02 / STRUCTURE

Turn observation into an inventory.

A useful inventory gives resources names, categories, locations, descriptions, and availability states.

03 / DECIDE

Understand before acquiring.

Identification comes before improvement, acquisition, allocation, valuation, or replacement.

Resource landscape

What counts as a resource?

Start broad. Resource identification becomes stronger when you deliberately look across physical, human, financial, technological, informational, and environmental dimensions.

Human resources

People can contribute much more than labor. Their skills, experience, relationships, judgment, availability, and local knowledge may be critical resources.

  • Skills
  • Experience
  • Volunteers
  • Expertise
  • Relationships
  • Local knowledge

Physical

Buildings, furniture, equipment, tools, vehicles, books, materials, land, and other tangible assets.

  • Equipment
  • Furniture
  • Buildings

Financial

Funds and financial capacity that can support an activity, project, service, or operational requirement.

  • Cash
  • Funds
  • Credit

Technological

Devices, connectivity, software, digital infrastructure, power systems, and technical capabilities.

  • Computers
  • Internet
  • Software

Informational

Records, documents, datasets, institutional knowledge, procedures, research, contacts, and useful intelligence.

  • Records
  • Data
  • Knowledge

Environmental

Land, water, natural surroundings, transportation access, community spaces, and other environmental conditions.

  • Land
  • Water
  • Space
The method

Six moves from scattered to visible.

The process is deliberately practical. It can begin with a phone, a simple table, and a clear question: what resources are already available?

01

Observe

Look at the environment without immediately judging or buying.

02

Inventory

Create a clear record of what appears to be available.

03

Categorize

Group resources so patterns and gaps become easier to see.

04

Locate

Record where each resource can actually be found or accessed.

05

Document

Give each resource enough detail to make the record useful.

06

Verify

Confirm that the resource exists, is accessible, and matches the record.
